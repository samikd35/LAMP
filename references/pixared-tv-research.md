**Brionvega Coro TVC 26 Monitor Pansound, circa 1983–85.** On the front of this burgundy laminate cabinet, a small door beside the LED display and power switch concealed the programme, colour and volume keys. On the Flip Set, the visitor would press the door marked **Customize** to change the landing letter or logo part. The transport and **Prepare MP4** key would be accessible with the door shut. [Museo Nazionale Scienza e Tecnologia, photographs and object record IGB-14974](https://collezioni-online.museoscienza.org/detail/IT-MUST-NTR001-018946/televisore-brionvega-coro-tvc-26-monitor-pansound)

**Sony Profeel KX-20HF1, 1980.** Sony housed the picture tube separately from the tuner and speakers. The Powerhouse collection photographs a 1983 Profeel system and identifies the monitor, tuner and remote. On the Cue Monitor, the visitor would press **Type a name** or **Upload a logo** on the separate tuner. The artwork would stay visible on the CRT while the visitor set the landing. [Sony Design gallery](https://www.sony.com/en/SonyInfo/design/gallery/KX-20HF1/) · [Powerhouse collection, object 86/106](https://collection.powerhouse.com.au/object/54270)

**Bang & Olufsen Beovision MX 2000, 1985.** David Lewis’s set reclined on a pivot foot. The 1986–87 catalogue shows both table positions and the coloured sidewall. That change of angle suggests a purpose for the Flip Set’s depth. Pressing **Enlarge** would bring the glass square to the viewer and give the current film the phone’s available area. With reduced motion the view would change directly. [Bang & Olufsen design history and product photograph](https://www.bang-olufsen.com/en/ch/story/years-of-magic#:~:text=1985%2C%20Beovision%20MX%202000) · [Bang & Olufsen’s 1986–87 catalogue, MX 2000 spread](https://retronik.silicium.org/DOCUMENTS/Audiovideo/Bang-Olufsen/Bang-Olufsen-Catalogue-1986-1987.pdf#page=20)
